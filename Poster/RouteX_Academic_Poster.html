Abstract & Problem Motivation

Navigation and autonomous logistics depend on shortest-path algorithms. While asymptotic bounds (e.g., $\mathcal{O}((V+E)\log V)$) are widely taught, empirical performance is profoundly dictated by spatial heuristic pruning, positive edge costs, and network topology.

RouteX creates a 100% offline, deterministic experimental platform to benchmark 5 graph algorithms across both synthetic 100+ node city topologies and real-world OpenStreetMap networks.

  • Zero API Dependencies: Eliminates tile server latency and geocoding rate limits.
  • Mathematical Purity: Compares algorithms on identical immutable graph snapshots.

Key Research Findings

  • A* Pruning Efficiency: A* reduces node expansions by 57.6% compared to Dijkstra while producing the exact same optimal distance ($6{,}560\text{ m}$).
  • Hop vs. Metric Trade-off: BFS produces the minimum number of intersections (4 turns), but its physical distance is $18.4\%$ longer than Dijkstra's.
  • Greedy Local Traps: Greedy Best-First Search commits to dead-ends near river embankments, demonstrating that greedily minimizing $h(n)$ without $g(n)$ fails near spatial obstacles.

Conclusion & Future Scope

RouteX bridges theoretical algorithm analysis and realistic spatial engineering. It proves that A* search is optimal for urban route planning under admissible heuristics.

Future Work: Time-dependent turn delays, dynamic traffic congestion matrices, and Contraction Hierarchies (CH) for nationwide graphs.
